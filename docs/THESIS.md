# Research Thesis — Human Profession Reconstruction

## Question

Can a meaningful portion of a human profession be reconstructed as portable machine infrastructure rather than being left implicit inside one person's training, memory, habits, institution, or a model prompt?

## Starting observation

A human specialist is not just:

`role + facts + skills`

Professional competence is usually produced by an interacting system:

`observe -> classify -> retrieve -> use tools -> follow procedures -> judge -> test -> document -> communicate -> hand off -> remember -> update practice`

That system is surrounded by institutional support:

`standards -> records -> permissions -> shared vocabulary -> training -> review -> escalation -> equipment -> libraries -> accountability -> continuity`

AXM Profession Fabric studies which parts can be made explicit, deterministic where useful, stateful, testable, source-grounded, and portable between intelligences.

## Core hypothesis

A large part of specialist performance may come from **professional machinery** that can be separated from the reasoning substrate.

If true, the same Professional Body could be inhabited by different intelligences and produce measurably more consistent professional behavior than those intelligences operating from a role prompt alone.

This is an experimental hypothesis, not an established fact.

## Three-layer model

### 1. Intelligence

The reasoning substrate. Examples may include a human, a frontier model, a local model, or a future machine intelligence.

The substrate contributes general reasoning, interpretation, abstraction, creativity, and adaptation. Its strengths and limits must remain visible.

### 2. Professional Body

The portable profession-specific machinery:

- bounded purpose and ownership;
- perception/input requirements;
- knowledge and source retrieval;
- procedures;
- tools;
- skills/capabilities;
- memory and state interfaces;
- evidence standards;
- decision rights;
- failure patterns;
- handoffs;
- professional tests;
- provenance and growth rules.

The body should make professional structure explicit enough that gaps can be diagnosed and repaired.

### 3. Institution

The environment in which Professional Bodies cooperate:

- canonical shared state;
- records and continuity;
- tool/knowledge services;
- coordination;
- review and disagreement handling;
- resource and permission systems;
- organizational memory;
- governance.

Profession Fabric does not assume the institution must be hierarchical or exclusively human/machine.

## What success would look like

Evidence for the hypothesis would accumulate if we can repeatedly show that:

1. a body performs representative tasks better or more consistently than a role-only baseline;
2. the same body remains useful when the underlying intelligence changes;
3. professional failures can be traced to missing/incorrect body structures rather than hidden prompt behavior;
4. new professional capability can be added with provenance, tests, and rollback;
5. multiple bodies can coordinate through explicit institutional interfaces without collapsing ownership boundaries;
6. a body can correctly abstain, escalate, dissent, or request missing evidence when that is the professional action;
7. long-running professional state survives individual model/chat turnover.

## What would falsify or weaken the hypothesis

The thesis should be weakened if:

- bodies add little beyond what a role prompt and strong model already provide;
- behavior is not portable between intelligences;
- deterministic machinery makes performance brittle without improving evidence or control;
- professional judgment cannot be usefully represented without hiding most competence back inside the model;
- maintenance cost grows faster than professional value;
- tests reward imitation of procedures while real task quality declines.

## Human boundary

The research is not premised on humans being obsolete.

Human professionals may contribute lived experience, embodiment, responsibility, relationships, cultural understanding, taste, creativity, moral judgment, and other capabilities that a machine body does not possess merely because it reproduces procedures.

The useful research question is not "can we pretend a model is a human?" It is:

> Which parts of accumulated professional practice can become explicit infrastructure, and how does that change what humans and machines can do together or independently?

## Research discipline

Do not design the unknown from imagination alone. Map real professional work, collect disagreement, observe failures, compile bounded structures, test them, and let evidence grow the body.
